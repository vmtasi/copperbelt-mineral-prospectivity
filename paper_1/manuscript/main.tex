\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Whether geological and geophysical predictor relationships remain predictive across geographically separated portions of a mineralized belt, and how those relationships vary spatially, is a central question for regional mineral prospectivity mapping. We address this question in the Central African Copperbelt using a Domain-Varying Hierarchical Model (DVHM), a partially pooled Bayesian logistic regression evaluated across four geographically contiguous held-out sectors. A compact global logistic baseline (CGLB) provides a comparator. The 1,872-cell modelling frame (nominal $5\,\mathrm{km} \times 5\,\mathrm{km}$ grid; 138 cells with a compiled occurrence record) includes distance to mapped faults, the minimum Euclidean distance to the nearest mapped internal lithological contact, Bouguer gravity, and retained surficial-lithology classes as predictors. Intercepts and the four distance coefficients (linear and quadratic, constructed from standardized distances) vary by Daly tectonic domain, while Bouguer gravity and surficial-lithology effects are global. Posterior draws provide uncertainty distributions for cell-level conditional occurrence probabilities. Predictor contributions to the out-of-fold linear predictor vary markedly across along-belt sectors: fault-distance contribution is highest in the northern sectors, while Bouguer gravity becomes increasingly important toward the south. Across four distance-representation specifications, mean ROC-AUC ranges from 0.688 to 0.719 and mean PR-AUC from 0.136 to 0.197. Distance associations recur across representations, whereas quadratic curvature is representation-dependent and the population-level stationary-point diagnostic $D^*$ is not identifiable. The analysis therefore reveals spatially heterogeneous fitted relationships between geological and geophysical predictors and prospectivity, together with heterogeneous geographic predictive transferability across the Copperbelt. These patterns are consistent with a spatially heterogeneous geological framework, while the statistical associations alone do not establish a specific causal geological mechanism or a universal distance relationship.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} mineral prospectivity mapping; Bayesian hierarchical model; spatial cross-validation; spatial heterogeneity; Central African Copperbelt; Daly tectonic domains; parameter uncertainty

\section{Introduction}

Regional mineral prospectivity mapping integrates geological, geochemical, and geophysical evidence to identify locations associated with known deposits. Published approaches range from knowledge-driven evidence integration \citep{BonhamCarter1994, Carranza2009} to logistic regression, weights of evidence \citep{BonhamCarter1989}, and machine-learning classifiers \citep{Porwal2006, Carranza2015, Ford2019, Yousefi2021, Matthieu2026}. A fundamental question is whether predictor--prospectivity relationships estimated in one part of a mineralized belt remain predictive in geographically separated sectors---and if not, how they vary spatially. In the Central African Copperbelt, regional variation in basin structure, stratigraphy, and ore assemblages \citep{Cailteux2005, Selley2005, Hitzman2012, Key2004, Ndonfack2025} suggests that a single set of global predictor weights may not adequately capture along-belt heterogeneity. Standard random cross-validation can underestimate prediction error when spatially proximate observations share covariates and occurrence histories \citep{Brenning2012, Roberts2017}; the magnitude of this bias depends on the prediction target and blocking design, and spatial blocks can create predictor extrapolation \citep{Valavi2019}.

We address this question using the Domain-Varying Hierarchical Model (DVHM), a partially pooled Bayesian logistic regression in which intercepts and distance-predictor coefficients vary by Daly tectonic domain \citep{Daly2025}, while Bouguer gravity and surficial-lithology effects are shared globally. Spatially varying coefficient structures provide a statistical representation of changing relationships without asserting causation \citep{Gelfand2003, Fotheringham2002}. The Bayesian formulation represents posterior uncertainty in parameters, hierarchical scales, and domain effects; for each held-out cell, posterior draws produce a distribution of conditional occurrence probabilities $p_i^{(s)} = \mathrm{logit}^{-1}(\eta_i^{(s)})$, summarized by the posterior mean, median, standard deviation, and 95\% credible interval. Uncertainty in evidence layers, model parameters, conditional probabilities, and outcomes are distinct quantities in prospectivity work \citep{Lisitsin2014, Parsa2021, Zuo2021}. Transfer is evaluated across four geographically contiguous along-belt sectors rather than through random cell splitting.

This study contributes (i) a partially pooled hierarchical Bayesian prospectivity framework evaluated under along-belt spatial separation, and (ii) a representation-sensitivity design that separates recurring proximity associations from representation-dependent polynomial curvature and $D^*$ stationary-point diagnostics. We ask how fault distance, lithological-contact distance, Bouguer gravity, and surficial lithology jointly relate to recorded occurrence labels; how model-component contributions differ across held-out sectors; how out-of-fold discrimination varies; and whether distance-response conclusions persist under alternative mathematical representations.

\section{Study area, spatial data, and evidence inventory}

\subsection{Study area and grid definition}

The study region encompasses the Central African Copperbelt across the border region of northern Zambia and the southern Democratic Republic of the Congo. The spatial frame is a nominal $5\,\mathrm{km}\times5\,\mathrm{km}$ grid in WGS 84 / UTM Zone 35S (EPSG:32735). A full 8,536-cell grid was generated and intersected with the union of the five USGS permissive-tract polygons, which are distributed in Arc 1950 Lambert Conformal Conic and were reprojected to EPSG:32735. The intersection retained 1,899 cells and clipped edge cells to the tract union; 253 cells retain the full $25\,\mathrm{km}^2$ area. The retained cells cover $21{,}790\,\mathrm{km}^2$ and span approximately $500\,\mathrm{km}$ along strike, with cell centroids from Easting $282{,}675$ to $715{,}465\,\mathrm{m}$ and Northing $8{,}380{,}251$ to $8{,}859{,}512\,\mathrm{m}$. The permissive tracts define the assessment frame; they are not a geological contact or the limit of mineralization.

The observational unit is one nominal $5\,\mathrm{km}\times5\,\mathrm{km}$ grid cell, represented by its clipped polygon, centroid coordinates $(x_i,y_i)$, and a binary compiled-record indicator $Y_i\in\{0,1\}$. The prepared table contains 1,899 cells and 139 positive labels. Twenty-seven cells have no stored domain assignment, including one positive cell; complete-case filtering and removal of unrecognized domain labels therefore yield 1,872 cells, including 138 positive and 1,734 background cells.

The response is cell-level presence, not an occurrence count: a cell is positive when at least one compiled USGS occurrence record falls within its polygon, and repeated records within a cell do not increase its label. The USGS Central African Copperbelt assessment database contains 187 point records: 102 prospects, 65 deposits, and 20 grouped records; grouped records represent multiple sites within 500\,m aggregated for resource modeling. Point-in-polygon assignment places 184 records inside the 1,899-cell frame and three outside; the 184 records map to 139 unique positive cells, of which 107 contain one record and the remainder contain two to five. Background cells indicate that no compiled record falls in the cell, not a verified absence of mineralization. The source metadata report a horizontal positional accuracy of 1,000\,m, source-map scales from 1:5,000 to 1:2,000,000, manual digitization for some sites, and a use constraint that the database not be displayed at scales larger than 1:1,000,000. A 1,000\,m positional uncertainty is one-fifth of the 5\,km cell size, so records near cell edges can be assigned to adjacent cells under a different location interpretation. Accordingly, fitted probabilities describe the conditional probability of a compiled cell label under the model and validation design, not a probability of discovery.

\paragraph{Distance to mapped faults.}
The fault predictor is the Euclidean distance from each cell centroid to the nearest digitized fault line in EPSG:32735. The archived distance table records one distance per cell, and the surviving 126-line layer attributes all lines to the Selley et al.\ (2005) Figure~1 compilation; the frozen values reproduce the archived table exactly. The fault raster carries a ten-point georeferencing fit recorded in a GDAL control-point file (fit residuals 0.6--1.6\,m in map coordinates), but the provenance of those control points and the positional accuracy of the underlying Figure~1 compilation are not stated in the available metadata. The 126-line layer reproduces 1,672 of the 1,899 frozen values, with the remaining 227 differing by up to about 33\,km, so the exact fault geometry used for the frozen predictor is not preserved. The distance is a cartographic proximity measure, not a surveyed distance.

\paragraph{Surficial lithology and contact distance.}
The lithology source is the Africa surficial lithology raster (RCMRD SERVIR-Africa Team and USGS; 3 arc-second, WGS 84; values 0--20, comprising 19 lithology classes plus water and an n/a code; NoData 255). Its metadata describe a compilation and reclassification of twelve digital geology, soil, and lithology databases, mixing bedrock and unconsolidated parent materials; it is not a bedrock host-rock map, and its spatial complexity and classification detail vary across Africa. In QGIS the raster was reprojected to EPSG:32735 at 93\,m and clipped to the tract union. The six classes present (metasedimentary, silicic, metaigneous, alluvium-fluvial, alluvium-saline, and water) match the class proportions of the source within the tract mask; a nearest-neighbour warp of the source to the same grid reproduces 99.82\% of valid pixels, consistent with nearest-neighbour resampling and no class reclassification, although the exact warp options were not recorded. Polygonization produced 314 polygons; after conversion to lines and removal of the outer boundary, 259 internal contact lines remained.

The contact predictor is the minimum Euclidean distance from each clipped cell centroid to any internal contact line in EPSG:32735; a cell whose centroid touches a contact has distance zero. The outer boundary of the tract union was removed before distance calculation, so the predictor measures internal lithological contacts only. The corrected one-row-per-cell table contains all 1,899 grid cells, with no missing or duplicate identifiers; its CSV and GeoPackage representations agree to better than $10^{-11}$\,m, and it is the single active source for the distance feature. The superseded many-to-many candidate table and its first-row-per-cell columns are retained only in the read-only historical archive and are not read by any active pipeline stage.

Each internal contact carries a single lithology-class attribute. Multi-point sampling on both sides of all 259 retained contacts resolves a different class on each side, and the stored class equals the class on the left side of the line's digitization direction in every case. The attribute is therefore a contact-side lithology, not the class at the cell centroid. The corrected distance reconstruction does not assign a cell class. The canonical training table retains the archived surficial-lithology class field unchanged as a secondary categorical attribute; that field is not reconstructed from the nearest-contact geometry and is not part of the distance definition. Contact-distance coefficients and response curves therefore describe the minimum internal-contact distance, while the class terms describe the archived surficial-lithology classes.

\paragraph{Bouguer gravity.}
The gravity predictor is a 2 arc-minute complete Bouguer anomaly grid (mGal; geographic longitude/latitude). The Bouguer and isostatic files carry a bare longitude--latitude--gravity header. The free-air, disturbance, and topography grids distributed with them carry BGI headers that name the WGM2012 free-air, disturbance, and ETOPO1 2 arc-minute products and use identical grid limits and spacing. The Bouguer grid is therefore identified as the WGM2012 complete Bouguer member from its companion files \citep{BGIWGM2012}; its own header names neither the product nor the datum. In QGIS, each cell centroid was transformed from EPSG:32735 to the grid's geographic coordinates and assigned the nearest node. All 1,899 frozen values match the source grid exactly, with nearest-node offsets of 0.07--2.56\,km (median 1.47\,km). No interpolation or offset correction was applied. EPSG:4326 follows the project configuration; the header does not state a datum.

\paragraph{Tectonic domains.}
The six domain labels follow the Daly et al.\ (2025) tectonic framework; a georeferenced Figure~14 raster, an 11-polygon layer, and a 45-line boundary compilation are preserved. The stored labels match the archived centroid--domain table for all 1,899 cells, including 27 nulls; the frozen model uses the stored labels for the 1,872 complete-case cells. Against the original hand-drawn polygons, 69 centroids fall outside all polygons, two fall in polygon overlaps, four non-null stored labels conflict with the single containing polygon, and one cell with a null stored label falls inside a polygon (76 affected cells). The 49 affected complete-case cells are all background, representing 2.8\% of the 1,734 background cells; none of the 138 complete-case positive cells is affected. The only affected positive cell (id 7074) has a null domain and lies outside the 1,872-cell complete-case frame. For the 69 gap cells, the distance to the nearest original polygon ranges from 26 to 4,906\,m (median 792\,m), and 29 carry the label of the nearest polygon. A repaired layer removes the four conflicts and the null-inside case; it leaves the same 27 nulls and the same two overlaps, with the stored label among the containing polygons in each overlap, and matches the stored labels for the remaining complete-case cells. These discrepancies are therefore best characterized as a cartographic linework limitation affecting some complete-case background cells without changing their stored labels; no complete-case positive assignment is affected. The repaired layer is a diagnostic reconstruction, not evidence of the original assignment, which is inferred to have been a centroid-in-polygon join with gap and overlap resolution.

\paragraph{Auxiliary layers.}
The archive also contains an isostatic gravity grid accompanying the Bouguer grid, an upward-continued magnetic grid, and mining-footprint counts. These layers were prepared during exploration but are not in the final DVHM feature set; the model uses fault distance, contact distance and class, Bouguer gravity, and the domain-varying terms.

\subsection{Domain-level positive-observation structure and partial-pooling qualification}

Cells with a compiled occurrence record in the modelling frame are concentrated exclusively in two of the six tectonic domains: 107 positive cells occur in NRB\_3a (out of 1,127 total cells) and 31 occur in NRB\_3b (out of 550 total cells). The remaining four domains contain zero recorded positive observations in this compilation: CRZ\_4b ($n=154$), NKB ($n=24$), MMSB ($n=16$), and SRB ($n=1$).

Methodologically, this empirical structure has specific implications for the hierarchical Bayesian model:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The formulation remains a fully partially pooled hierarchical model across all six domains, with domain-varying parameters linked through shared population hyperparameters $\mu_p$ and $\sigma_p$.
\item In CRZ\_4b, NKB, SRB, and MMSB, the likelihood contains no within-domain positive-class information. Their domain-specific effects are informed by the available negative observations together with the shared hierarchical population structure, and therefore rely more strongly on partial pooling than effects in domains containing both classes.
\item Consequently, domain-specific coefficient interpretations for CRZ\_4b, NKB, SRB, and MMSB must be treated with substantial caution, and standard binary classification metrics such as ROC-AUC are undefined within these single-class partitions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Complete preprocessing chain}

The overall modelling sequence is summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:model_workflow}. The prepared spatial frame contains 5 km grid cells with six Daly-domain categories (27 cells are unassigned). The four contiguous folds are assigned before model fitting. For each fold, continuous predictors are standardized using the training partition only, and the resulting transformation is applied unchanged to both training and held-out cells. Quadratic fault- and contact-distance terms are formed from the standardized values. Lithology categories are retained only when represented by both positive and background cells in the training partition; held-out data do not determine that support.

\subsection{Distinction between validation folds and tectonic domains}

This study uses two distinct spatial stratifications:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Along-belt validation folds (Folds 1--4):} Centroid coordinates are subjected to PCA; observations are projected onto the first principal component, sorted by that projection, and divided into four approximately equal contiguous groups of 468 cells. Each group is held out once to evaluate geographic transfer. This reduces local train-test overlap but does not guarantee independence across fold boundaries.
\item \textbf{Daly tectonic domains (6 domains):} A geological and structural stratification based on Katangan basin evolution \citep{Daly2025}, used as the grouping variable for hierarchical partial pooling of model parameters.
\end{itemize}
The model is trained on three spatial folds and evaluated on the held-out fold; domain-level performance metrics are computed post hoc as descriptive strata.

\section{Bayesian hierarchical methodology and spatial validation}

\subsection{Domain-varying hierarchical model specification}

For cell $i$ in Daly domain $d(i) \in \{1,\dots,6\}$, the DVHM is a partially pooled hierarchical Bayesian logistic regression \citep{GelmanHill2007}:

\begin{equation}
Y_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_i),\qquad \mathrm{logit}(p_i) = \eta_i,
\end{equation}

where the linear predictor $\eta_i$ decomposes as:

\begin{equation}
\eta_i = \alpha_{d(i)} + \beta_{f,\mathrm{lin},d(i)} z_{f,i} + \beta_{f,\mathrm{sq},d(i)} z_{f,i}^2 + \beta_{l,\mathrm{lin},d(i)} z_{l,i} + \beta_{l,\mathrm{sq},d(i)} z_{l,i}^2 + \beta_g z_{g,i} + \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{rock},i}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{rock}}.
\end{equation}

The model distinguishes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Domain-varying parameters:} Domain intercepts $\alpha_d$ and the four distance coefficients (fault linear $\beta_{f,\mathrm{lin},d}$, fault quadratic $\beta_{f,\mathrm{sq},d}$, lithological contact linear $\beta_{l,\mathrm{lin},d}$, and lithological contact quadratic $\beta_{l,\mathrm{sq},d}$).
\item \textbf{Global parameters:} The Bouguer gravity coefficient $\beta_g$ and the surficial-lithology vector $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{rock}}$ are shared globally across all domains.
\end{itemize}

Domain-varying parameters use a non-centred hierarchical parameterization to ensure efficient MCMC exploration:

\begin{equation}
\beta_{p,d} = \mu_p + \sigma_p \cdot \mathrm{offset}_{p,d}, \qquad \text{for } p \in \{f_{\mathrm{lin}}, f_{\mathrm{sq}}, l_{\mathrm{lin}}, l_{\mathrm{sq}}\},
\end{equation}

and

\begin{equation}
\alpha_d = \mu_\alpha + \sigma_\alpha \cdot \mathrm{offset}_{\alpha,d}.
\end{equation}

The model uses the following priors:

\begin{align}
\mu_p &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1), & \sigma_p &\sim \mathrm{HalfNormal}(1),\\
\mathrm{offset}_{p,d} &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1), & \mu_\alpha &\sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathrm{logit}(\hat{p}_{\mathrm{train}}), 1\right),\\
\sigma_\alpha &\sim \mathrm{HalfNormal}(1), & \mathrm{offset}_{\alpha,d} &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1),\\
\beta_g &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1), & \boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{rock}} &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}),
\end{align}

where $\hat{p}_{\mathrm{train}} = \sum y_{\mathrm{train}} / N_{\mathrm{train}}$ anchors the intercept prior to the empirical training base rate.

\subsection{Continuous probability prediction and threshold-free evaluation}

For each posterior draw $s \in \{1,\dots,S\}$ and held-out cell $i$, the draw-specific parameters and the held-out cell's fold-preprocessed features determine its conditional prospectivity probability:

\begin{equation}
\eta_i^{(s)} = \alpha_{d(i)}^{(s)} + \dots, \qquad p_i^{(s)} = \mathrm{logit}^{-1}(\eta_i^{(s)}) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-\eta_i^{(s)})}.
\end{equation}

The reported out-of-fold prediction is the posterior mean conditional probability:

\begin{equation}
\bar{p}_i = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^S p_i^{(s)}.
\end{equation}

No classification threshold is applied to generate predictions or compute discrimination metrics. Performance is evaluated across the continuous distribution of predicted probabilities using rank discrimination metrics that do not require a threshold \citep{Lobo2008}.

\subsection{Uncertainty concepts}

Four distinct uncertainty concepts apply to this analysis:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Parameter posterior uncertainty:} Represented by MCMC posterior samples of coefficients and hyperparameters.
\item \textbf{Posterior uncertainty in the conditional probability:} For each held-out cell, the posterior distribution of $p_i$ is induced by parameter uncertainty. Posterior means, medians, standard deviations, and 95\% credible intervals quantify uncertainty in $p_i$, not whether a deposit will occur.
\item \textbf{Future outcome uncertainty:} The workflow produces conditional probability estimates, not simulated Bernoulli realizations $Y_i^{(s)} \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_i^{(s)})$.
\item \textbf{Performance metric uncertainty:} Fold and pooled metric intervals use paired row-bootstrap resampling of fixed OOF scores; a separate multiscale spatial block bootstrap evaluates robustness of $\Delta$AUC at specified block scales.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout the results and discussion, fitted coefficients and predictor-component shares describe posterior statistical associations, not causal geological effects. This distinction is stated once here and applies to all subsequent reporting.

\subsection{Decomposition of linear predictor variance}

To describe how additive predictor components vary across held-out cells in each sector, the out-of-fold linear predictor is decomposed for each test cell $i$ in fold $k$:

\begin{equation}
\eta_i = c_{\mathrm{intercept},i} + c_{\mathrm{fault},i} + c_{\mathrm{lith},i} + c_{\mathrm{grav},i} + c_{\mathrm{rocks},i},
\end{equation}

where each component represents the posterior expectation across retained MCMC draws:
\begin{align}
c_{\mathrm{fault},i} &= \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{post}}\!\left[\beta_{f,\mathrm{lin},d(i)} z_{f,i} + \beta_{f,\mathrm{sq},d(i)} z_{f,i}^2\right],\\
c_{\mathrm{lith},i} &= \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{post}}\!\left[\beta_{l,\mathrm{lin},d(i)} z_{l,i} + \beta_{l,\mathrm{sq},d(i)} z_{l,i}^2\right],\\
c_{\mathrm{grav},i} &= \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{post}}\!\left[\beta_g z_{g,i}\right],\\
c_{\mathrm{rocks},i} &= \mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{post}}\!\left[\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{rock},i}^{\mathsf T} \boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{rock}}\right].
\end{align}

For each predictor component $m \in \{\mathrm{fault}, \mathrm{lith}, \mathrm{grav}, \mathrm{rocks}\}$, its dispersion across the $N_{\mathrm{test}}$ held-out cells of fold $k$ is measured by its sample standard deviation:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SD}_m = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{test}}} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}^{(k)}} (c_{m,i} - \bar{c}_m)^2}.
\end{equation}

The reported component share is the SD-normalized share of additive-component dispersion:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Share}_m = \frac{\mathrm{SD}_m}{\mathrm{SD}_{\mathrm{fault}} + \mathrm{SD}_{\mathrm{lith}} + \mathrm{SD}_{\mathrm{grav}} + \mathrm{SD}_{\mathrm{rocks}}} \times 100\%.
\end{equation}

These shares describe relative dispersion of additive components across held-out cells and are not comparable to proportions of variance explained.

\subsection{Distance-representation sensitivity design and robustness criteria}

To test whether distance-response inference depends on mathematical representation, four models form a $2\times 2$ factorial grid:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering

\label{tab:sensitivity_design}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & Quadratic & Linear \\
\midrule
Raw distance ($z$) & Model A (DVHM) & Model C \\
Log distance ($\log(1+x_{\mathrm{km}})$) & Model B & Model D \\

Model A is the primary DVHM specification with raw quadratic distance terms; Models B--D are the corresponding sensitivity variants. Models A and C use standardized raw distance; Models B and D use standardized $\log(1+x_{\mathrm{km}})$.

As a non-hierarchical comparator, a compact global logistic baseline (CGLB) is fitted separately within each training fold under the same four-fold design. The CGLB contains standardized Bouguer gravity and lithological-contact distance with its quadratic term; it includes neither fault distance nor domain-varying parameters.

Across the four spatial folds, six Daly domains, and two distance predictors (fault and lithological contact), there are $4 \times 6 \times 2 = 48$ evaluable comparison cases. In each case, Model A (raw-quadratic) is compared against Model B (log-quadratic) using three pre-registered criteria:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Criterion 1 (Curvature probability agreement):} The absolute difference in posterior probability of positive quadratic curvature between representations must be less than $0.15$:
\begin{equation}
\left| P(\beta_{\mathrm{sq}} > 0 \mid \text{Model A}) - P(\beta_{\mathrm{sq}} > 0 \mid \text{Model B}) \right| < 0.15.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Criterion 2 ($D^*$ median agreement):} The relative difference between posterior median stationary points $D^*_A$ and $D^*_B$ (in physical kilometers) must not exceed 25\%:
\begin{equation}
\frac{|D^*_A - D^*_B|}{\max(|D^*_A|, |D^*_B|)} \le 0.25.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Criterion 3 (Data support concentration):} Both median stationary points must fall within their respective training data empirical support ranges:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{support\_min}_A \le D^*_A \le \mathrm{support\_max}_A \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{support\_min}_B \le D^*_B \le \mathrm{support\_max}_B.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

A relationship is classified as representationally robust if and only if it satisfies all three criteria simultaneously.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Spatial predictive transferability and prospectivity mapping}

The DVHM out-of-fold predictions produce the regional prospectivity map in Figure~\ref{fig:oof_prospectivity}. Across the four folds, DVHM ROC-AUC values are 0.846, 0.709, 0.687, and 0.605, and PR-AUC values are 0.110, 0.126, 0.147, and 0.163. Pooled across all 1,872 cells, DVHM ROC-AUC is 0.707 (95\% bootstrap CI: 0.667--0.744), compared with 0.559 (0.509--0.615) for CGLB, a gain of $+0.147$ ($+0.073$ to $+0.217$).

Pooled performance masks geographic divergence. In Fold 2, the DVHM trails the CGLB by $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}=-0.118$ (95\% paired row-bootstrap CI $[-0.224,-0.011]$); in Fold 3, the DVHM exceeds the CGLB by $+0.056$ (95\% CI $[-0.002,+0.112]$), an interval that includes zero. The corresponding intervals for Folds 1 and 4 also span zero.

In secondary domain stratification, NRB\_3a DVHM ROC-AUC is $0.682$ (95\% CI $[0.636,0.724]$) versus $0.577$ for CGLB ($[0.533,0.629]$; $\Delta=+0.105$). In NRB\_3b, DVHM ROC-AUC is $0.609$ ($[0.519,0.699]$) versus $0.878$ ($[0.827,0.920]$; $\Delta=-0.269$). ROC-AUC is undefined in CRZ, NKB, SRB, and MMSB because these strata contain no positive observations.

A separate multiscale spatial block bootstrap resampled occupied coordinate blocks at $10\times10$, $15\times15$, $20\times20$, and $25\times25$ grid scales. The pooled $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}$ interval excluded zero at the $15\times15$, $20\times20$, and $25\times25$ scales (for example, $+0.146$ with 95\% interval $[+0.048,+0.238]$ at $25\times25$) but included zero at $10\times10$ ($[-0.015,+0.277]$). Fold 2 remained negative at the $15\times15$ and $25\times25$ scales. These results apply to the evaluated block definitions and do not replace the fixed-score row-bootstrap intervals.

\subsection{Multivariate posterior structure}

How do predictor associations differ across domains? In NRB\_3a, lithological-contact distance has negative linear coefficient medians ($-0.977$ to $-0.618$) and positive quadratic coefficient medians ($+0.352$ to $+0.394$), with posterior probabilities of positive curvature $P(\beta_{l,\mathrm{sq}} > 0) \ge 0.998$ across all four folds. In NRB\_3b, posterior support for positive curvature is fold-dependent and weaker than in NRB\_3a (0.766--0.896).

Fault-distance effects vary more dramatically. In NRB\_3a, linear medians range from $-2.622$ to $-0.705$, while quadratic medians are negative in Fold 2 ($-0.018$) and positive in Folds 1, 3, and 4 ($+0.380$, $+0.690$, $+1.627$). In NRB\_3b, fault linear medians shift to positive values ($+0.091$ to $+1.435$), demonstrating that the directional association with fault distance is not spatially uniform across the belt.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Predictor associations and conditional inference}

The multivariate results establish that geological and geophysical predictors contain joint predictive signal in the Copperbelt modelling frame. Fault distance, lithological-contact distance, Bouguer gravity, and surficial lithology contribute jointly to the fitted out-of-fold predictor. The hierarchical structure permits domain-varying intercepts and distance effects while sharing information across Daly domains through partial pooling; individual coefficients therefore reflect associations conditional on the other predictors. The contact-distance predictor is the minimum Euclidean distance to the nearest mapped internal lithological contact, with the outer study-area perimeter excluded; contact-specific coefficients are therefore interpreted as associations with that internal-contact distance.

\subsection{Spatial non-stationarity and geographic transferability}

The OOF decomposition shows that predictor-component shares shift systematically along the belt: fault distance dominates in the north, while Bouguer gravity becomes co-dominant in the south. The regional geological literature documents corresponding variation in basin structure and stratigraphic setting \citep{Cailteux2005, Selley2005, Hitzman2012, Ndonfack2025}, providing context for interpreting these patterns.

Spatially separated prediction demonstrates that pooled belt-wide skill can conceal regional performance divergence. The DVHM outperforms the CGLB in Fold 3 but underperforms it in Fold 2, reinforcing the necessity of spatially separated validation strategies \citep{Roberts2017, Valavi2019}. Daly's tectonic domain classification \citep{Daly2025} provides a principled geological stratification for hierarchical partial pooling, but the analysis estimates empirical associations within the compiled data and does not constitute a formal test of Daly's tectonic hypothesis.

\subsection{Representation sensitivity and model parsimony}

Proximity associations persist across mathematical transformations, but polynomial curvature and the derived stationary point $D^*$ are sensitive to functional form. The near-zero pass rate (1 of 48 cases) demonstrates that $D^*$ is a model-dependent algebraic property of quadratic curves rather than an invariant physical quantity, and prospectivity mapping should avoid translating quadratic extrema into drilling targets.

The quadratic distance term improved mean ROC-AUC under raw distance (Model A $0.712$ vs Model C $0.688$) but not under log distance (Model B $0.704$ vs Model D $0.719$). Linear and logarithmic representations are simpler specifications; however, the results do not establish a universally preferred distance form.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several methodological limitations apply:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Sample size and domain imbalance:} Only 138 cells carry a compiled occurrence label, with 107 concentrated in NRB\_3a and 31 in NRB\_3b. Four domains contain zero positive labels, precluding within-domain discrimination evaluation.
\item \textbf{Residual spatial autocorrelation:} Contiguous spatial blocking mitigates optimistic cross-validation bias, but spatial autocorrelation may persist across block boundaries \citep{Roberts2017}.
\item \textbf{Input layer uncertainty:} Regional geological maps, structural compilations, and gravity grids contain measurement, interpolation, and cartographic errors that propagate into distance calculations; the gravity product identity is inferred from the headers of its companion BGI grids rather than from the Bouguer file itself.
\item \textbf{Contact-distance predictor:} The contact distance is the minimum Euclidean distance from each cell centroid to the nearest mapped internal lithological contact, with the outer study-area perimeter excluded. It is a two-dimensional cartographic proximity measure: it does not represent subsurface contact geometry, contact dip, or the three-dimensional distribution of mineralized horizons. The archived surficial-lithology class is retained only as a secondary categorical attribute and is not reconstructed from the nearest-contact geometry. Contact-specific coefficients and response curves therefore describe associations with the mapped internal-contact distance, not a causal distance to mineralization.
\item \textbf{Surface proxies vs.\ 3D geometry:} Near-surface distance features do not capture three-dimensional fault dip, subsurface stratigraphy, or depth to basement.
\item \textbf{Discrete blocking approximation:} Four discrete spatial folds approximate a continuous along-belt metallogenic gradient.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}

The DVHM provides uncertainty-aware out-of-fold conditional-probability estimates for 1,872 modelling cells across the Central African Copperbelt. Posterior uncertainty is propagated to each held-out cell, yielding posterior means, standard deviations, and 95\% credible intervals for conditional occurrence probabilities.

The OOF decomposition reveals geographically varying predictor-component shares: fault-distance dominance in the northern sectors gives way to co-dominant Bouguer gravity in the south. Fold-level and domain-level discrimination vary substantially, demonstrating that a pooled belt-wide metric can conceal regional performance divergence.

Distance associations recur across the evaluated representations, but quadratic curvature and the derived $D^*$ diagnostics are representation-dependent, and population-level $D^*$ is not identifiable. Spatially separated validation evaluates transfer among the specified folds but does not guarantee transfer to all frontier areas.

\section{Reproducibility, data, and code availability}

The analyses use PyMC \citep{Salvatier2016}, ArviZ, and scikit-learn. The DVHM was estimated using two chains, 2,500 tuning steps, and 1,500 retained draws per chain, with target acceptance 0.99 and random seed 42. Code, available prepared data, and derived results are provided at \url{https://github.com/vmtasi/copperbelt-mineral-prospectivity}. The USGS occurrence compilation, the Bouguer gravity grid, and the Africa surficial lithology raster are cited above but are not redistributed with the repository. The modelling frame is reproducible as the intersection of the 5\,km grid with the five USGS permissive tracts. The frozen fault, gravity, and occurrence values are numerically reproduced from archived layers. The contact distance is the minimum Euclidean distance from each grid-cell centroid to the nearest mapped internal lithological contact in EPSG:32735, with the outer study-area perimeter excluded; the corrected table contains exactly one row per cell, and its CSV and GeoPackage values agree to numerical precision. The archived surficial-lithology class is retained as a secondary categorical attribute and was not reconstructed. The superseded candidate table and its first-row-per-cell columns are preserved only in the read-only historical archive. The exact QGIS operation sequence, the provenance and accuracy of the fault-map control points, and the domain polygon digitization and gap/overlap resolution were not recorded in the available processing history. These limits should be considered when interpreting the spatial predictors; they do not alter the frozen model inputs or predictions reported here.
