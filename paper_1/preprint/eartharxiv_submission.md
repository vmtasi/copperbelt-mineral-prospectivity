**Title**
Rankings Transfer but Base Rates Do Not: Leave-One-Sector-Out Evaluation of a Prospectivity Model along the Central African Copperbelt

**Abstract**
Mineral prospectivity models are usually validated where they were trained but used to rank ground elsewhere. We evaluated how a Bayesian prospectivity model transfers between four contiguous sectors of the Central African Copperbelt, and why it fails. The model relates the presence of a compiled occurrence record in 1,872 nominal 5 km cells (138 positive) to distance to faults, distance to lithological contacts and Bouguer gravity, with a complementary log-log link and cell-area offset. All analyses were pre-specified and all models fitted by Markov chain Monte Carlo. Trained on three sectors, the model ranked the cells of the fourth with a pooled ROC-AUC of 0.721 (95% interval 0.687–0.755) and sector values of 0.727–0.834, against 0.781 under random cross-validation. Probabilities did not transfer: the south-eastern, Zambian sector was over-predicted (mean predicted probability 0.130 against an observed rate of 0.019) and the north-western sector under-predicted (0.081 against 0.130), under every model variant and partition. Oracle decompositions attribute most of the south-eastern loss to a base-rate shift that an intercept correction would remove (+0.098 log-score units per cell). Rescaling the slopes added little, and a locally refitted model helped marginally, and only in the sector that straddles the Zambia–Democratic Republic of the Congo border. Contact distance transferred, Bouguer gravity was informative only in the south-east, and the fault-distance association depended on uneven fault-line coverage; these predictor-level conclusions changed with the sector boundaries. Transferred models should be used for ranking, recalibrated before their probabilities are compared between areas, and validated on held-out regions reported separately.
